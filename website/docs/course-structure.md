# Course Structure

This course is structured as a comprehensive 15-week semester program, with each module building upon previous concepts to develop a deep understanding of Physical AI and humanoid robotics. The progression moves from foundational principles to advanced applications, culminating in capstone project work.

## Module 1: Foundations of Physical AI (Weeks 1-3)

**Module Title**: Introduction to Physical AI and Embodied Intelligence

**Conceptual Focus**:
- Distinction between traditional AI and Physical AI
- Principles of embodied cognition and situated intelligence
- Physical interaction as a fundamental aspect of intelligence
- Historical perspectives on embodied vs. symbolic AI approaches

**Learning Emphasis**:
- Understanding the theoretical foundations of Physical AI
- Analyzing the role of environmental interaction in intelligent behavior
- Examining case studies of physical intelligence in natural systems

## Module 2: Embodied Cognition and Intelligence (Weeks 4-6)

**Module Title**: Embodied Cognition and Sensorimotor Integration

**Conceptual Focus**:
- Morphological computation and passive dynamics
- Sensorimotor coupling and feedback loops
- The role of body in cognitive processes
- Affordances and environmental interaction

**Learning Emphasis**:
- Analyzing how physical form influences perception and action
- Understanding the relationship between body structure and cognitive capabilities
- Evaluating different approaches to embodied cognitive architectures

## Module 3: Humanoid Robot Design Principles (Weeks 7-9)

**Module Title**: Anthropomorphic Design and Biomechanics

**Conceptual Focus**:
- Biomechanical principles underlying human movement
- Advantages and challenges of humanoid form
- Degrees of freedom and kinematic structures
- Anthropomorphic vs. alternative design approaches

**Learning Emphasis**:
- Evaluating design trade-offs in humanoid robot construction
- Understanding biomechanical constraints and capabilities
- Analyzing the relationship between form and function in humanoid systems

## Module 4: Perception and Control in Physical Systems (Weeks 10-12)

**Module Title**: Sensory Processing and Motor Control

**Conceptual Focus**:
- Multi-sensory integration in physical environments
- Feedback and feedforward control strategies
- State estimation in uncertain environments
- Real-time control challenges and solutions

**Learning Emphasis**:
- Applying control theory to physical robot systems
- Analyzing sensory processing for real-world interaction
- Understanding the challenges of real-time physical interaction

## Module 5: Learning in Physical Systems (Weeks 13-14)

**Module Title**: Adaptive Behavior and Skill Acquisition

**Conceptual Focus**:
- Learning paradigms for embodied systems
- Motor skill acquisition and refinement
- Adaptation to environmental changes
- Transfer learning in physical contexts

**Learning Emphasis**:
- Understanding how physical systems acquire and refine behaviors
- Analyzing different approaches to learning in uncertain physical environments
- Evaluating the role of experience in developing physical intelligence

## Module 6: Applications and Integration (Week 15)

**Module Title**: Capstone Applications and Future Directions

**Conceptual Focus**:
- Integration of concepts across all modules
- Real-world applications of humanoid robotics
- Ethical considerations and societal impact
- Current research frontiers and future directions

**Learning Emphasis**:
- Synthesizing knowledge from all course modules
- Applying theoretical concepts to practical scenarios
- Critically evaluating current research and identifying future opportunities

## Weekly Structure

Each week typically includes:
- Two 75-minute lectures focusing on theoretical concepts
- One 50-minute discussion section for analysis and debate
- Optional laboratory sessions for hands-on exploration of concepts
- Reading assignments from primary literature and course materials

## Assessment Integration

Assessment is distributed across the modules to reinforce learning objectives:
- Weekly problem sets connecting theory to application
- Mid-term examination covering foundational concepts
- Research paper analysis and presentation
- Final project integrating concepts from multiple modules

This structure ensures a logical progression from fundamental concepts to advanced applications, with each module building upon previous knowledge while preparing students for subsequent material.
